# Historical backtest results: 2022 and 2024

First run of `scripts/run_historical_backtest.py --cycles 2022,2024`
(2026-08-11), under the corrected control-based x_D/x_R decomposition
(`docs/methodology.md`). Per spec Section 26 (minimum viable research
project): this is the run that decides whether "the near-zero aggregate
Nash result replicates."

## Headline exploitability table (spec Section 22, output #3)

| Cycle | Budget D | Budget R | RegretD | RegretR | E (exploitability) | E as % of E[D seats] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $99.5M | $91.4M | +2.35 seats | +3.55 seats | **+5.90 seats** | 2.74% |
| 2024 | $102.1M | $47.2M | +2.36 seats | +3.23 seats | **+5.59 seats** | 2.58% |

**Replicates.** RegretD is nearly identical across cycles (2.35 vs. 2.36);
RegretR is the same order of magnitude and same direction in both
(RegretR > RegretD both times) despite 2022's budgets being far more even
between the parties than 2024's. Two independent cycles landing on the same
qualitative and near-identical quantitative picture is exactly the spec's
bar for treating this as a real pattern rather than a one-cycle artifact.

## Nash equilibrium

| Cycle | Converged | Rounds | E[D seats] obs → Nash | Multi-start agreement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | Yes | 10 | 214.85 → 214.69 | Yes (max pairwise diff <$6.7K, all 3 starts converge to the same fixed point) |
| 2024 | Yes | 13 | 217.04 → 217.20 | Yes (max pairwise diff <$4.6K) |

Aggregate expected seats barely moves from observed to Nash in either
cycle -- consistent with the old trilogy's near-zero two-sided result
(project_spec.md Section 2's motivating observation) replicating under this
project's own, independently-built two-player machinery and the corrected
budget scope.

**But allocation distance is large, not small**: L1 distance between
observed and Nash allocation is $326.7M (2022) and $292.6M (2024) --
roughly 86-98% of the theoretical MAXIMUM possible L1 distance given each
side's budget (2×budget_D + 2×budget_R). Read together with the near-zero
seat-value gap, this says the objective has a wide, flat region near its
optimum: many quite different race-by-race allocations achieve almost the
same aggregate expected-seat value, so "observed ≈ Nash in seats" does NOT
imply "observed ≈ Nash in dollars." Spec Section 22's output #5 explicitly
asks for both metrics side by side for exactly this reason -- H3
("observed allocations are substantially closer to Nash than to unilateral
optima") needs the L1/ranking-overlap comparison against unilateral optima
and Cook-heuristic/random benchmarks before it can be evaluated properly;
that comparison isn't built yet (see `docs/methodology.md`'s Level D row).

## Persistent strategic value (spec Sections 13-14)

Top-6-by-|Z| races per side, isolated baseline, **restricted to races with
real current party spend** (`> $10,000`) before ranking by `|Z|`, and
`retention_rate` reported as `NaN` when `|V_uni| < 0.001` expected seats
rather than as a misleading percentage. Both restrictions were added after
investigating a retention>100% anomaly the FIRST pass of this run produced
-- see `docs/methodology.md`'s writeup for the full trace. Short version:
the unrestricted candidate pool was dominated by $0-party-spend races (379
of 433 for D), where MSG sits at a known-unstable boundary point of the
persuasion-ceiling curve; every flagged race in the original run had
`party_d_obs == $0`, confirmed directly.

**Corrected results**:

| Cycle | Side | Currently-funded races | Retention range (material V_uni only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | D | 64/433 | 92%-131% (5 of 6 material; CT-05 below materiality bar) |
| 2022 | R | 78/433 | 71%-160% (4 of 6 material; MI-04, CA-47 below bar) |
| 2024 | D | 54/433 | 76%-210% (all 6 material) |
| 2024 | R | 39/433 | all 6 below materiality bar (|V_uni| <= 0.0002 seats) |

Most races now cluster close to 100% retention (FL-27 2022: 96.6%; WI-03
2022: 100.9%; WI-01 2024: 100.7%; OR-04 2024: 104.7%) -- i.e. the apparent
unilateral opportunity mostly survives the opponent's optimal response
essentially intact, a materially different and more interpretable picture
than either the original degenerate run (all races collapsing to
-RegretR/-RegretD) or the retention>100%-dominated first attempt at the
isolated baseline.

A few D-side races still show genuine retention above 100% even after both
fixes (NV-01 2024: 210%, NH-01 2024: 194%) -- traced analytically and
confirmed NOT a bug: a real second-order effect of how the specific
delta-financing reallocation changes R's whole-portfolio best response
under the shared budget constraint (see `docs/methodology.md`). 2024's
R-side result -- EVERY currently-funded top-|Z_R| race falls below the
materiality bar -- is itself informative: among races NRCC actually funds,
allocation is already close to locally optimal, consistent with
"Republican opportunity" being the smallest quadrant bucket in both cycles.

## What this run does and doesn't establish

Per spec Section 27's decision gate: condition A (observed allocations
consistently close to Nash, multiple cycles) is **not** supported as
written -- the seat-value gap is small but the allocation-distance is
large, which is a more nuanced result than "close to Nash." Condition B
(low aggregate exploitability + a stable persistent-value subset) has
better support after the retention-anomaly investigation: aggregate E is
small relative to the two-party seat total (~2.6-2.7%) and replicates, and
among races DCCC/NRCC actually fund, most of the apparent unilateral
opportunity survives optimal opponent response near-intact (~100%
retention), which is itself a real, replicated-shape finding, not a
methodological artifact. Next step: the Level D five-way benchmark
comparison (equal allocation / Cook heuristic / one-sided optimizer / Nash
/ random feasible) that would actually settle H3.
