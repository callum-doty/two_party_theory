# Historical backtest results: 2022 and 2024

Refreshed 2026-08-12 under the corrected, symmetric two-player payoff
(`docs/methodology.md`'s "Shared probability model: fixed baseline, signed
saturation" and subsequent sections) -- supersedes the original 2026-08-11
run, which used a D-anchored formula for reporting alongside an
uncalibrated mirrored-ceiling search for `BR_R`. Exploitability, race
taxonomy, and PSV below are exact (SLSQP, `game/best_response.py`); the Nash
equilibrium section uses the validated concave-envelope surrogate
(`game/best_response_surrogate.py`, ~500-1,000x faster than SLSQP, checked
against exact solves within 0.03-0.10 expected seats) to run a far more
thorough 3-start x 2,000-round search than SLSQP could feasibly complete --
see `docs/methodology.md` for the validation and the warm-start caveat this
run's own regret check needed.

Per spec Section 26 (minimum viable research project): this is the run that
decides whether "the near-zero aggregate Nash result replicates."

## Headline exploitability table (spec Section 22, output #3)

| Cycle | Budget D | Budget R | RegretD | RegretR | E (exploitability) | E as % of E[D seats] |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $99.5M | $91.4M | +3.03 seats | +2.41 seats | **+5.44 seats** | 2.53% |
| 2024 | $102.1M | $47.2M | +2.84 seats | +2.30 seats | **+5.14 seats** | 2.37% |

**Replicates, with the asymmetry REVERSED from the original run.** RegretD
is nearly identical across cycles (3.03 vs. 2.84) and is now the LARGER term
in both -- the original run's headline (`RegretR > RegretD` both times,
"Republicans have more to gain than Democrats") does not survive the fixed
ceiling. That asymmetry was itself partly an artifact of the old mirrored
ceiling being looser than the fixed, two-sided one both sides now share:
RegretD barely moved (2.35/2.36 -> 3.03/2.84), since D's side was never the
one exploiting an unregularized extrapolation; RegretR fell by roughly a
third (3.55/3.23 -> 2.41/2.30). Two independent cycles landing on the same
qualitative and near-identical quantitative picture is still exactly the
spec's bar for treating this as a real pattern, just a different pattern
than originally reported.

## Nash equilibrium

`docs/methodology.md`'s "Nash equilibrium: a real limit cycle" section has
the full investigation (two damping regimes, up to 300 rounds, exact
SLSQP). Summarized here with the deeper surrogate-based check added:

| Cycle | Converged | Rounds x starts | E[D seats] obs -> orbit | Multi-start agreement | L1 distance obs -> orbit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | **No** | 2,000 x 3 | 215.17 -> 216.44-216.48 | Tight but not exact: e_seats within 0.04 across starts, max pairwise allocation diff $1.0-3.1M | $355.0M |
| 2024 | **No** | 2,000 x 3 | 217.17 -> 218.57-218.59 | Tight but not exact: e_seats within 0.02 across starts, max pairwise allocation diff $1.3-1.9M | $292.9M |

The original run reported clean convergence (`converged: Yes`, 3-start
agreement within ~$5-7K, 10-13 rounds) -- that result does NOT reproduce
under the corrected payoff. Damped Gauss-Seidel best-response dynamics
instead settle into a small, bounded, non-shrinking oscillation: exact-SLSQP
regret at a warm-started check of the 2,000-round fixed point is **+0.19
(D) / +0.25 (R) seats for 2024** and **+0.32 (D) / +0.44 (R) seats for
2022** -- small relative to the observed-allocation regrets above, but never
zero, and unmoved by 10x more rounds or three different starting points,
which all converge to nearly the same aggregate value and orbit a shared,
tight region rather than diverging or landing on a single point. Read
together, this says: the near-zero aggregate seat gap from unilateral
optimization (the original trilogy's motivating finding) still replicates
in the loose sense that E[D seats] barely moves --
but it moves toward a small oscillating band, not a clean fixed point,
and "Nash equilibrium" in the strict zero-regret sense does not appear to
exist where these dynamics can reach it. `project_spec.md` Section 12's
"presence of cycles" diagnostic is doing real work here, not turning up
empty.

**Allocation distance is still large, not small**: L1 distance between
observed and the Nash-dynamics orbit is $355.0M (2022) and $292.9M (2024)
-- consistent with the original run's finding that the objective has a
wide, flat region near its optimum, now confirmed under a genuinely
regularized, symmetric payoff rather than one side's unbounded formula.
"Observed ≈ near-equilibrium orbit in seats" still does NOT imply "observed
≈ near-equilibrium orbit in dollars." H3 ("observed allocations are
substantially closer to Nash than to unilateral optima") still needs the
Level D five-way benchmark comparison (`docs/methodology.md`'s validation
table) before it can be evaluated properly -- not built yet.

## Race taxonomy (spec Section 8)

| Quadrant | 2022 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Possible over-capitalization | 197 | 186 |
| Democratic opportunity | 103 | 125 |
| Under-contested / escalation pressure | 73 | 52 |
| Republican opportunity | **60** | **70** |
| Locally equilibrated | 0 | 0 |

"Republican opportunity" was the smallest bucket in the original run (15 in
both cycles); under the fixed ceiling it is now the second-largest D-side-
comparable bucket in 2024 (70, more than "under-contested"), and roughly
quadrupled in 2022 (15 -> 60). The shadow price `lambda_R` (the median
marginal value among interior races at R's own unilateral optimum) is
computed from a materially different search now -- the old mirrored ceiling
and the new fixed, symmetric one do not just rescale RegretR, they change
which specific races BR_R treats as interior versus corner-bound, so this
shift is expected rather than a red flag. Descriptive only (spec Section
8's own caveat) until checked against explicit best-response calculations
race-by-race, which PSV below partially does for a small subset.

## Persistent strategic value (spec Sections 13-14)

Top-6-by-|Z| races per side, isolated baseline, restricted to races with
real current party spend (`> $10,000`) before ranking by `|Z|` -- same two
guardrails as the original run (`docs/methodology.md`'s PSV retention
writeup), still necessary and unaffected by the ceiling fix (they address a
selection artifact in which races get ranked, not the payoff formula
itself). Funded-race counts are UNCHANGED from the original run (64/433 D,
78/433 R for 2022; 54/433 D, 39/433 R for 2024) -- the control-budget
scoping that determines who's funded didn't change, only the payoff
governing what happens when they deviate.

| Cycle | Side | Currently-funded races | Retention range (material V_uni only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | D | 64/433 | 60%-98% (5 of 6 material; NY-17 below materiality bar) |
| 2022 | R | 78/433 | 33%-105% (5 of 6 material; MI-04, NY-17 below bar) |
| 2024 | D | 54/433 | 58%-112% (5 of 6 material; WA-03 below materiality bar) |
| 2024 | R | 39/433 | all 6 below materiality bar (`|V_uni| <= 0.0006` seats) |

The top-6-by-|Z| SELECTION shifted somewhat under the new ceiling (different
races now rank highest by |Z|), so only races appearing in BOTH the
original and this run's picks give a clean before/after comparison --
checked directly against both runs' raw output below, not assumed from the
retention-range shift alone:

- **2022 D-side** (5 common races): NJ-03 131% -> 87%, AZ-02 117% -> 60%,
  WI-03 101% -> 97%, FL-27 97% -> 98%, AZ-06 96% -> 96% (unchanged).
- **2022 R-side** (3 common races): FL-15 100% -> 50%, NY-20 71% -> 33%,
  MI-04 NaN -> NaN (still immaterial both times).
- **2024 D-side** (5 common races): NV-01 209% -> 58%, NH-01 194% -> 72%,
  CT-05 120% -> 112%, WI-01 101% -> 99%, TX-34 76% -> 95%.
- **2024 R-side** (3 common races): CA-27, NY-19, CA-13 all NaN -> NaN
  (still immaterial both times).

The pattern is a compression toward the middle, not a uniform drop: the
original run's most EXTREME retention values -- both the >150% outliers
(NV-01 209%, NH-01 194%, NJ-03 131%, AZ-02 117%) and, in one case, a
below-100% value (TX-34 76%) -- move toward 60-100% under the properly
regularized ceiling, while values already near 100% (FL-27, WI-03, AZ-06,
WI-01) stay roughly where they were. This is consistent with the ceiling
fix's own mechanism: the old formula let a single race's deviation induce
an outsized, partly-unregularized swing in the opponent's whole-portfolio
reoptimization (in either direction -- eroding OR amplifying the original
opportunity past what the fixed, bounded ceiling now permits), and the
fixed ceiling caps that swing symmetrically for both sides.

2024's R-side result is unchanged in kind from the original run: EVERY
currently-funded top-|Z_R| race still falls below the materiality bar
(`|V_uni|` between 0.00009 and 0.0006 seats) -- among races NRCC actually
funds, allocation is already close to locally optimal under either payoff
version, consistent with "Republican opportunity" still being a real but
now larger quadrant bucket (opportunities exist, they're just not
concentrated in the specific races R is CURRENTLY funding).

## What this run does and doesn't establish

Per spec Section 27's decision gate: condition A (observed allocations
consistently close to Nash, multiple cycles) is still **not** supported as
written, now for a sharper reason than before -- there is no longer a clean
Nash fixed point to be "close to" under damped best-response dynamics, just
a bounded oscillating region the dynamics never fully enter. Condition B
(low aggregate exploitability + a stable persistent-value subset) has
similar support to before, with the specific numbers changed: aggregate E
is still small relative to the two-party seat total (~2.4-2.5%, was
~2.6-2.7%) and still replicates, but the persistent-value subset shows
MORE erosion under the corrected ceiling than the original run found,
particularly on the R side -- "most opportunity survives near-intact" is a
weaker claim now than the original ~100%-clustered retention picture
suggested. Two clear next steps: (1) the Level D five-way benchmark
comparison (equal allocation / Cook heuristic / one-sided optimizer / Nash
/ random feasible) that would actually settle H3, now against a payoff
that's actually safe to search with on both sides; (2) characterizing the
Nash limit cycle itself -- does a mixed-strategy equilibrium exist, and is
the game's non-convergence under Gauss-Seidel dynamics a property worth
reporting as a first-class finding rather than a solved diagnostic
(`docs/methodology.md`'s "Known open items" has the open questions).
